# 128 — v0.1 ships without `goto` within twice Git's time

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-11
- **Agenda topic:** none (getting v0.1 ready: clean code and the gate)
- **Decided by:** the owner (2026-11-11). The limit `goto` is held to until v0.2 was left to Claude to pick from the runs on record: [decision 129](129-gotos-limit-until-v0.2-and-the-status-gate-enforced.md).
- **Supersedes:** part of [decision 099](099-a-third-speed-gate-within-twice-gits-time.md): that each of its three steps, `goto` among them, must take at most twice Git's time for v0.1 to ship, and that the job fails when any of the three is over twice.

## Context

Decision 099 holds Bolt to twice Git's time on Git's own source, for three steps, on GitHub's Linux and Windows runners, and says v0.1 ships when all three pass; decision 108 compares the medians of ten runs. After milestone 6, `status` with nothing changed and a small save pass on both systems, and so does going back to the first version on Windows. Going back on Linux doesn't: across the ten runs on record it took 1.79 to 2.65 times Git's time, and was over twice in eight ([benchmarks.md](../benchmarks.md) §8 and §9, and the Nightly run of 2026-11-11 on `main`, [42966403404](https://github.com/WisHonor/Bolt/actions/runs/42966403404): 31.3 ms against Git's 11.8, 2.651 times).

What it would take is known (decision 109): a `goto` flushes ten times, where Git's checkout flushes none, and the walk runs on one thread. Flushing less changes what a crash can leave behind, and a walk on several threads re-shapes the module that decides what is saved; neither belongs in the last steps before a first release.

## Options

- **A. Hold v0.1 until `goto` passes on Linux.** It waits on one of the two changes above.
- **B. Ship v0.1 with `status` and the small save held to twice Git's time, and `goto` printed** with a limit that catches a real regression, the twice for v0.2.
- **C. Drop the third gate.** Nothing would hold the everyday steps to Git any more.

## Decision

**B.**

- **v0.1 ships without `goto` meeting the third gate.** `status` with nothing changed and a small change saved stay held to twice Git's time (the medians of ten runs, decision 108), on GitHub's Linux and Windows runners; the job fails when either is over.
- **`goto` within twice Git's time is a goal for v0.2.** Until then the job prints its ratio, says it is over the goal when it is, and fails only past a limit picked from the runs on record, so that a real regression still shows ([decision 129](129-gotos-limit-until-v0.2-and-the-status-gate-enforced.md) picks it).
- Everything else in decisions 099 and 108 stands: the data, the steps, the runners, the untimed preparation, ten runs after a warm-up, the medians, and the means, best and worst runs printed beside them.

## Consequences

- `bench/run.sh gate`, the `speed-gate` job and its description in [testing.md](../testing.md) follow; [benchmarks.md](../benchmarks.md) §1 states the rule, and "v0.1 ships when" in [milestones.md](../milestones.md) says what is checked.
- The release notes say that going back to another version takes about twice Git's time on Linux, and more on some machines; they already say so.
- Decision 099 gets a back-link line.
